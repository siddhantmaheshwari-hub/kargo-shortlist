KARGO CV SCORING RUBRIC (v2)
Product Manager (PM) and Senior Product Manager (SPM)
Built to avoid false rejections
=====================================================================

Why this rubric looks the way it does
-------------------------------------
Most of Kargo's best hires would fail a typical screen. Rohan, Sunita,
Aditya and Meghna were all rated Exceeds Expectations, and none held a
PM title. So this rubric scores BEHAVIOUR WHEREVER IT APPEARS (any role,
any function, any industry) and routes borderline candidates to a second
look instead of a rejection.

Every criterion traces to specific lines in the past-hire profiles
(Exceeds: Rohan, Sunita, Aditya, Meghna, Lavanya; Meets/Below: Vikram,
Rahul, Preetham).


=====================================================================
HOW TO SCREEN ONE CV (about 5 minutes)
=====================================================================

1. Eligibility check. The only knockout is: Mumbai-based or willing to
   relocate. Nothing else ends a candidate's chances at this stage.

2. Score each criterion 0-3 using the anchors below. Score only what the
   CV states.

3. If the CV hints at something but is unclear, give it a 1 and mark it
   "?". Never give a 0 for something vaguely described. A 0 means the
   behaviour is absent, not unclear.

4. Calculate the total:
      (score / 3) x weight, summed across the four criteria.

5. Apply the bands and override rules.

6. Write down the CV phrase that justifies each score. This becomes the
   record of why each candidate was shortlisted or declined.

NEVER SCORE: titles, years of experience, degree, company names,
certifications, tool lists, or adjectives in the summary.


=====================================================================
THE FOUR CRITERIA
=====================================================================

A PM-level 3 counts as a 2 on the SPM rubric. The "3 (SPM)" line shows
the extra bar needed for a 3 on the SPM rubric.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
A. HANDS-ON OPERATIONS EXPOSURE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

0        No operational role at all (product, tech or marketing only).
         Reference: Vikram, Rahul.

1        Worked on operations from a desk (integrations, analytics,
         dashboards) without handling transactions.
         Reference: Preetham (logistics APIs).

2        Handled live transactions in any operations-heavy domain, but
         no volumes or counterparties are named.

3 (PM)   Names volumes AND counterparties (carriers, CHAs, customs,
         ports, clients).
         Reference: Rohan "180+ shipments monthly";
         Lavanya "800+ shipments monthly".

3 (SPM)  The PM-level 3, plus they were the coordination point across
         3 or more types of external party.
         Reference: Sunita "Primary coordination point between...
         customs brokers (CHAs), freight agents, port authorities,
         and the DGFT office".

Counts:    Pre-PM careers, family businesses, consulting, and CS or
           sales roles with an operations load. Freight, warehousing,
           FMCG distribution, field ops, fulfilment and pharma logistics
           all qualify.

Look for:  "shipments monthly", "coordinated with", "holds",
           "exceptions", "clearance", named counterparties.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
B. UNPROMPTED FIX, ADOPTED BY OTHERS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

0        Nothing built beyond assigned work.

1        Built something, but it was in their assigned remit or there
         is no adoption evidence.
         Reference: Preetham (monitoring dashboard); Rahul (case
         studies).

2        Unprompted fix, adopted within their own team only.
         Reference: Vikram (PRD template).

3 (PM)   Triggered by a breakdown they noticed, then adopted by others,
         with a number or "became standard".
         Reference: Rohan's tracker "adopted across the 12-member
         operations team within two weeks".

3 (SPM)  The fix outgrew where it started: it became a platform
         feature, or other teams or clients adopted it.
         Reference: Rohan's BoL module "now a core platform feature";
         Lavanya's dashboard "adopted by 2 other regional teams".

Look for:  "after noticing/finding", "adopted by", "became the
           standard", "now used by", "retained permanently".


---------------------------------------------------------------------
C. RESOLVES LIVE BREAKDOWNS PERSONALLY
---------------------------------------------------------------------

0        No incident mentioned.
         Reference: Vikram, Rahul.

1        Generic claims like "on-call" or "escalation management" with
         no specific case.

2        A specific incident resolved within their assigned role or
         rota, with an outcome.
         Reference: Preetham (coupon bug patched within 48 hours).

3 (PM)   An unscheduled breakdown they owned end to end, with the
         outcome stated.
         Reference: Meghna (customs hold resolved overnight, shipment
         departed on schedule); Sunita (workflow rebuilt over a weekend
         when the FMS vendor changed format without notice).

3 (SPM)  A high-stakes, hard-to-reverse event (migration, cutover,
         outage, inspection) where they were the final decision-maker,
         with the consequence stated.
         Reference: Rohan (vendor migration "with no data loss");
         Lavanya (outage post-mortem owned to closure).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
D. ABSORBS EXTRA LOAD WITHOUT ASKING FOR MORE PEOPLE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

0        Nothing, or they grew their team to handle the load.
         Reference: Rahul (hired a team of 4).

1        Claims like "high-volume" or "fast-paced" only.

2        Took on extra scope, but it was brief or no outcome is stated.
         Reference: Lavanya (sole PM across 3 product areas).

3 (PM)   Explicitly took on extra volume or accounts with no added
         headcount, and the outcome held.
         Reference: Meghna (8 extra accounts for 3 months, "no churn").

3 (SPM)  The same, sustained for 6 months or more.
         Reference: Rohan ("18-month period... no additional headcount
         added").


=====================================================================
WEIGHTS AND BANDS
=====================================================================

              A      B      C      D     Shortlist  Second look  Decline
PM           30%    30%    25%    15%      >= 65      40 - 64      < 40
SPM          25%    30%    30%    15%      >= 70      45 - 69      < 45

Weights in each row add up to 100%.


=====================================================================
OVERRIDE RULES (these stop good CVs being auto-rejected)
=====================================================================

1. SPIKE RULE: A 3 on criterion A or B guarantees at least a second
   look, whatever the total.

2. UNCLEAR RULE: Two or more "?" marks means a second look, whatever
   the total.

3. CROSS-ROUTE RULE: An SPM applicant who misses the SPM shortlist but
   scores 65 or more on the PM rubric goes to the PM shortlist. A PM
   applicant who scores 70 or more on the SPM rubric gets flagged for
   SPM.

4. NO HARD FILTERS on years, title, degree or industry label, including
   the year ranges in the job descriptions.

SECOND LOOK means: a second reviewer reads the full CV, re-scores only
the "?" items, and records the decision.

=====================================================================
INTERVIEW PROBES (use when a score is 1 or "?")
=====================================================================

A  "Walk me through a normal day in that operations role. Who did you
   deal with, and how many shipments or cases?"

B  "What's something you built that nobody asked for? Who uses it now?"

C  "Tell me about the worst thing that broke on your watch. What did you
   do in the first hour?"

D  "When did your workload jump without extra people? What did you
   change to cope?"
